Fact Vault

🔍

    From idea to posted Short in about 20 minutes

    Every idea in the vault is a complete package: a spoken script in 4–6 scenes, a footage search for each scene, the title, description, tags, hashtags, thumbnail text, a pinned-comment idea and the sources the fact was checked against. Scripts run 60–85 words, which is 25–35 seconds spoken: the length that gets replayed.

    1. Pick and copy

    Choose an idea, press Copy script. Mark it Used when you post it, so the list keeps track for you (this is saved in this browser only).

    2. Voice

    Record the script yourself in one take, or use a text-to-speech voice: CapCut's text-to-speech, Clipchamp's voices and ElevenLabs' free tier all work. Scene 1 is the hook; say it fast and plain, no wind-up.

    3. Footage

    Each scene has a footage search in monospace. Click it to open that search on Pexels (free, no credit needed), or paste it into Pixabay. One clip per scene, vertical if you can find it; otherwise crop a landscape clip to 9:16.

    4. Edit

    1. CapCut (free): new project, 9:16, 1080 × 1920.
    2. Drop the voice track in first, then one clip per scene, cut on the sentence.
    3. Auto captions, big and centred in the middle third (the top and bottom are covered by YouTube's interface).
    4. Keep the thumbnail text on screen for the first second if you like; it is written to fit in 2–3 lines.
    5. Export 1080p, 30 fps. Total length 25–35 seconds.

    5. Post

    Press Copy post text: it gives you the title, description, hashtags and the tag list in one paste. Post at the same times every day (one to three Shorts a day beats ten), and put the pinned comment under the video yourself.

    6. Make a hundred more

    The scripts were written to a fixed rulebook. Paste this into ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini to get more ideas in the same shape; paste your used titles at the bottom so it never repeats one.

    You are the writers' room for a YouTube Shorts facts channel. Write 10 new ideas as a JSON list. Each idea has: topic (animals, space, science, history, human body), title, scenes, description, tags, hashtags, thumbnail_text, pinned_comment, sources.

Rules for the fact: surprising, specific and TRUE. Verify each one with web search in two reputable sources (university, museum, NASA/ESA, peer-reviewed paper, major encyclopaedia or news outlet) and put the best 1–2 URLs in sources; drop anything you cannot verify. No medical or financial advice, nothing about named private people, nothing graphic. Prefer physical, visible events with a number (a strike, a bite, a crash, something eaten, frozen, crushed, surviving the impossible) and animals with a superpower. No abstract comparisons, no geography processes, no "did you know your body" trivia.

Rules for the script (scenes): 4–6 scenes, 60–85 words in total, 25–32 seconds spoken. Scene 1 is the hook: at most 12 words, the fact itself in plain words with a concrete noun and a number, no "did you know". Scenes 2–3: the proof (a number, a comparison, who found it). Second-to-last scene: why it happens, one sentence. Last scene: a twist or a bigger number that hands the viewer back to the hook, never a summary. Short spoken sentences, contractions, "you" where natural. Write numbers the way they are spoken. Each scene has keywords: 2–4 concrete visual nouns for a stock-footage search (for example "mantis shrimp strike aquarium"), and scene 1's keywords must be the single most dramatic footage that exists for the fact. No emojis.

Rules for the metadata: title 35–70 characters, curiosity-driven, carries the number or the verb, no ALL CAPS, no hashtags. description 2–4 lines: line 1 restates the hook, lines 2–3 add one detail, last line asks a question. tags: 10–15 plain tags mixing the specific subject, the category and broad terms like "mind blowing facts". hashtags: 3–5 including #shorts. thumbnail_text: at most 45 characters. pinned_comment: a sharp question or a bonus fact, no hashtags.
